*Part 2 of the Work Graph series. A meeting is the place where most projects actually get decided, and most of it is stored in the calendar of whoever organized it.*

A client call ends. Someone typed notes into a document on their laptop. Someone else recorded the session. A third person has a half-remembered account of what the client said about the launch date. All three are accurate, and none of them is on the project. Two weeks later, a colleague asks what the client's position was, and the answer depends on whom they happen to ask.

A meeting is a project event, and it is stored as a personal one.

## Four ways a meeting goes missing

### 1. The Private Notes Problem

Notes live where the note-taker keeps them. They are good notes, and they are invisible to everyone who was not in the room, and often to the person who took them six months later.

### 2. The Recording Nobody Replays

A transcript exists, and it is an hour of text with no structure. Nobody opens it because finding one sentence in it costs more than asking a colleague.

### 3. The Series With No Memory

A weekly status call is one meeting repeated forty times. Each occurrence is treated as new, so the context from last week has to be rebuilt from nothing.

### 4. The Guest List That Left With the Invite

Who attended, which client they represented and who was only copied are all in the invite, and the invite is in someone's mailbox.

## A meeting as a record

With the Work Graph, a meeting is a first-class record on a project. It has a title and a date, its **attendees** (with the people from the client's side kept distinct from your own team), the **series** it belongs to if it repeats, and the project it is about. Its **transcript** or **notes** are attached to it, so the content stays with the event.

You can bring the content in the way that suits the meeting. Add notes directly. Upload a transcript from the recording tool your team already uses. The meeting then sits in the project's **Work** tab alongside the project's decisions and tasks, visible to the members who are allowed to see it.

This is exactly why we built the Work Graph around the project instead of around a person: the meeting belongs to the engagement, so it should live there.

## Talk about a meeting

Once a meeting is a record, a conversation can use it. In any project conversation you can **mention a meeting** and the assistant works from it. You can also **start a conversation from a meeting**, with the meeting already attached, so that "what did the client say about the rollout" is asked from the meeting page and answered from its content. Nothing is sent until you send it.

Because the record has structure, the answers are better than a search across text. The assistant knows who was there, which series the meeting belongs to, and what else on the project it connects to.

## What you stay in control of

**Visibility follows the project.** A meeting is visible to the people who can see its project. A meeting attached to a restricted project is not visible to a member outside it.

**The content stays where it is allowed to.** The Work Graph stores pointers and metadata about a meeting. When Parle quotes a passage, it reads the content live with the permissions of the person asking.

**Deleting means deleting.** A meeting can be removed, and Parle remembers that it was removed so that it is not quietly brought back. The mechanism is explained in [Your data, your control](/blog/2027/01/05/your-data-your-control/).
