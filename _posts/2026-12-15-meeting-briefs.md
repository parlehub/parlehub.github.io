*Part 6 of Project IQ series. The best five minutes before a meeting are the ones you do not spend hunting for context.*

Ninety seconds before a client call, a project lead opens a calendar invite. The invite says who is coming and nothing else. The notes from last time are in a document somebody wrote. The open actions are in two places. The proposal that changed on Friday is in a library with forty similar files. The lead walks in with a general sense of the project and a specific fear of being asked about something they have not read.

Preparation is a search problem that nobody has time to run.

## Why people walk in underprepared

### 1. The Scatter

Everything a person needs to prepare exists. It is in the calendar, a notes document, a task tool and a shared library, and gathering it is a task of its own.

### 2. The Missing Memory

For a recurring meeting, what matters is what changed since the last one. The invite does not know there was a last one.

### 3. The Ask-Around

The fallback is to message a colleague. It works, and it costs the colleague's time, and it is not repeatable.

## One page for one meeting

Every meeting in Project IQ has a **brief**, reached from a **Brief** button on the meeting page and on upcoming meetings in the project's meetings list. It is a single page with four sections:

**Who's coming.** The attendees with their role and, for external attendees, the client they represent. The brief shows no "last met" date and no count for any person.

**Last time.** Up to the last three related meetings, each with its confirmed decisions and the current status of its action items. Related means the same series first; if there is no series, the meetings on the same project with the most attendees in common.

**Open actions.** The actions owned by the people coming, as a list of items. It is a list of work, not a tally per person.

**Key documents.** Up to five documents added or changed since the last related meeting, with a short summary where one exists. If more were changed, the page says how many it left out.

Where there is little to show, the brief says so plainly. A first meeting in a series says "This is the first meeting in this series." It does not fill the gap with plausible filler.

## Two buttons, and no model call

At the top of the page are two actions. **Chat about this meeting** opens a new conversation with the meeting already attached, ready for a question. **Add notes** opens the dialog for attaching notes or a transcript.

Opening the brief itself does not call a model. It is assembled from records Project IQ already holds, at the moment you open it, from what you are allowed to see. The assistant's own meeting-brief tool uses the **same assembler** with a smaller cap on its response, so the page and the agent never disagree about what was open or what had changed.

## Shown only what you could already see

A brief is built per person, per request. A decision made in a meeting you cannot see does not appear, and the omitted counts never hint that it exists. A meeting you are not allowed to see answers as not found. The rules are the same ones described in [Who can see what](/blog/2026/12/29/who-can-see-what/).

## Why we built it

Preparing for a meeting should be reading, not searching. The brief turns the connected record of a project into something a person can take in during the walk to the room, and it makes the first minutes of every meeting less about reconstructing the last one.

## Availability

Meeting briefs are available with Project IQ on Business Elite and Enterprise. If Project IQ is switched off for your organization, the brief page explains that, and the meeting and its content remain. Sending a brief to attendees before the meeting is not part of this release.
