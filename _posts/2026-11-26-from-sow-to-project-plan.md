*Part 1 of the Work Graph series. The contract already says what you owe the client and by when. Parle reads it and proposes the plan; you decide what stands.*

Every engagement starts with a document that defines it, and almost every engagement then ignores that document for the first week. Someone reads the statement of work, copies the dates into a tracker, retypes the deliverables into a project plan, and guesses at the milestones. The copy is slightly different from the original. Three months later, when a client asks whether a deliverable was in scope, the team reopens the PDF to find out.

*Why does the most important document on the project feed nothing else?*

## Where the plan goes wrong

### 1. The Retyping Tax

A project manager spends the first days of an engagement turning prose into rows. Each row is a chance to drop a deliverable, misread a date or round off a condition. Nobody checks the retyped plan against the contract again until there is a dispute.

### 2. The Orphaned Deliverable

A deliverable lives in the SOW as a sentence. In the tracker it becomes a task with no memory of the sentence it came from. When the client later says that was never agreed, the team cannot show where the commitment was written.

### 3. The Plan That Never Had an Owner

When the plan is typed by whoever had time, it carries no record of who accepted it or when. A deliverable is delivered, a client signs it off, and the only evidence is an email.

## Start the plan from the contract

Parle lets you add a project's SOW when you create the project, or later from the **Work** tab of the project. You can upload the document or pick it from the SharePoint or Google Drive library your project is already linked to. The SOW then has a home on the project: you can replace it, download it or remove it, and see where its extraction stands.

When the document arrives, Parle reads it and **proposes** the project's start date, end date, milestones and deliverables. Nothing is applied automatically. The proposals wait for a person to review them, accept the ones that are right, and correct the ones that are not. A deliverable that Parle cannot place is left for a person to place; it is not invented.

This is precisely the problem we built the Work Graph to solve. The SOW is the root of the project's record, and what you accept from it becomes part of the project's connected history.

## What you end up with

**Deliverables as records, not sentences.** Each accepted deliverable is a named, dated item on the project. The deliverables view shows each one with its status, its due date, who accepted it and how much of the work behind it is done.

**A plan that traces back to the contract.** Because the deliverables came from the SOW, the link is kept. Anyone looking at a deliverable can see the document it was proposed from, which is the answer to the scope dispute before it starts.

**Dates the whole project agrees on.** The start date, end date and milestones you accepted appear in the project timeline and on the project dashboard, so the calendar in the plan is the calendar in the contract.

**Review before anything counts.** The model's job is to read quickly. A person's job is to decide. Suggested items are marked as suggested until someone confirms them, and the same rule applies to everything Parle proposes across the Work Graph.

## What it does not do

It does not replace judgment about a badly drafted SOW. If a document is vague about a date, the proposal will be vague too, and a person will need to settle it. It also does not push the plan into other tools on its own.
